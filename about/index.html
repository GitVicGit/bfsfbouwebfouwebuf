Biography

Victor Guerin (b. 1993) is a French-British artist and engineer who lives and works between London and Coutances, Normandy. Working across sculpture, installation, painting and moving image, he examines how human intervention reshapes ecosystems. His material research brings industrial residues into contact with organic matter, making transformation and material memory tangible while questioning the conditions under which ecological resilience is possible. He completed an MA in Sculpture at the Royal College of Art in 2024, following an MEng in mechanical and industrial engineering at Arts et Métiers ParisTech and an MSc in operational research at Lancaster University, both in 2016.

Selected exhibitions include ArtHouse Jersey (commission, 2026); Saatchi Gallery, London (2025); the Royal Scottish Academy, Edinburgh (2025, 2024); neimënster, Luxembourg (residency and commission, 2025); Dalkeith Palace, Dalkeith (2024); the New Zealand Academy of Fine Arts, Wellington (2024); Liangzhu Archaeological Site, Hangzhou (residency and accession, 2023). Other residencies include ArtHouse Jersey, Jersey (2025) and Sir John Soane’s Museum, London (2024).

Guerin received the Normandy Region’s MILLENIUM Grant (2026) and was a finalist for the Hyundai Award for Excellence in Sustainability and Creative Practice (2024). In 2025, he joined ArtLoft’s inaugural London fellowship cohort and was selected by the Franco-British Council as a Young Leader.

Artist statement

I begin with materials at the threshold between resource and residue: salvaged bitumen and concrete, weathered wood, oxidised metal, stone, soil and collected matter. I treat them as records of extraction, use, weathering and dispersal. Across sculpture, installation, painting and moving image, I bring industrial residue into contact with organic matter and treat transformation as a form of material memory, testing the limits of resilience in damaged environments.

My training as an engineer taught me to isolate variables, optimise systems and model flows. In the studio, I turn that logic against its promise of control, constructing conditions in which weight, weathering, growth and contamination continue to shape the work. Material research drives this process, while scientific inquiry enters through translation: a wildfire report becomes a score, colour becomes a measure and gathered residue becomes an archive.

The work develops where construction meets weathering and attraction cannot be separated from toxicity. I examine how human intervention reshapes ecosystems by making slow or dispersed processes – combustion, erosion, accumulation and chemical transfer – perceptible through surface, sound, colour and scale. Rather than explain or resolve these systems, the work holds their material evidence and uncertainty together.

Environmental responsibility

The world is facing an interconnected environmental crisis shaped by climate change, biodiversity loss, pollution, and material overconsumption. As an artist, this affects the conditions in which I work, the materials I use and the systems on which I rely.

My work engages with structure, material behaviour, extraction, erosion, reuse and the tensions between resilience and fragility. Because these concerns are embedded in the practice itself, environmental responsibility is part of how I conceive, fabricate and present my work. I recognise that artistic production has real environmental impacts, from material extraction and energy use in fabrication to transport, installation, packing and studio operations. No practice is perfectly sustainable, so I approach responsibility as an ongoing process of assessment, reduction and improvement.

I prioritise reclaimed, salvaged, recycled and low-impact materials where appropriate, make work with longevity and future reuse in mind, and reduce unnecessary waste in production and installation. I favour local sourcing and fabrication, lower-emission transport options, while considering the embodied environmental cost of materials and processes from the point of design onwards.

My aim is to develop a rigorous and accountable way of working in which artistic ambition and environmental responsibility are treated as mutually reinforcing.
